Here is Emma Lazarus rewritten in acid: the same sonnet you memorized in school, its warmth inverted so the torch-bearer becomes "an amoral man" whose "flame is as poisoned lightning." It's a bold conceit, and PCBender lets the words do the heavy lifting, opening with sparse piano tracing that slow minor cycle before a spoken-baritone lead eases in, unhurried despite the 143 BPM count. The pre-chorus modulation into E major is the hinge that makes the whole thing move; you feel the harmonic ground shift under "Father of Exiles" and it earns the release. Where the track truly opens up is the long final stretch past the three-minute mark, when the plain "Alas, poor Lazarus / Look at where we've come" refrain swells into a gospel-tinged wall of strings and backing harmonies that turns lament into something closer to a hymn. The verses can feel more recited than sung, and the density arrives so late that the first half asks for patience, but that restraint is the point: it saves everything for the flood. A highlight, and one that rewards a listen with the lyric sheet in hand.
