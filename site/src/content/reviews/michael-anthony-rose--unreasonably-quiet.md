Rose builds this one on a 3/4 pulse at a brisk clip, which is the first surprise: a waltz that never asks you to sway, driven instead by a Hammond groove and gritty guitar working against the meter rather than settling into it. The lyric is the thorniest he's written since the abstractions of 'Prepositions,' turning a Greek word for a woven basket into a meditation on what we hold and what we let fall through, and when the chorus lands on 'Unreasonably quiet and silently loud,' the paradox pays off in the arrangement itself, dense and swelling one moment, spacious the next. His deep baritone rides the middle of it comfortably, and the instrumental stretch around the four-minute mark, that spiral into organ and guitar before the final chorus returns, gives the five-plus minutes room to breathe rather than sprawl. It asks more of a listener than 'Them Bones' did, and the payoff comes on the second or third pass rather than the first: a grower, but a rewarding one for those willing to sit with it.
