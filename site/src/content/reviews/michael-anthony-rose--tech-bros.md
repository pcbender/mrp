The cloned witness of First Things First hands directly to the machines, and Tech Bros is the widescreen swing that mostly connects. Rose builds a corporate-coup radio play at a hard clip in F minor, letting the executives sing their fast, glossy counterpoint ("We built the search / We built the cloud") before a blunt baritone strips their power line by line: "You boys built the cage. / I brought the lock." The pre-chorus catalog, that clipped "Search / Payment / Message / Face," works like a metronome for the surveillance state, and the machine choir chorus lands its hook cleanly on "The future belongs to the lock." The masterstroke is placement: after five minutes of synth grid and gated drums, the plain piano arrives with the Nanny's retrospective, and "The youngest asked for apple juice / A kitchen panel placed the order" does more quiet damage than the whole industrial build around it, the human witness surviving even into the machine age. At five and a half minutes it asks a lot, and the sheer density of voices means a few turns blur on first pass; this is a grower, the kind of concept piece that rewards a second sit with the words in front of you. Think of it as Rose's dystopian set-piece, closer to a Ministry track scored by a diarist than anything on the quieter end of his catalog.
