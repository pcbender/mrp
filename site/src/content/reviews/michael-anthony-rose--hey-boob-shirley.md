Where "Municipal Tongue Clamp" turned its satire on institutions, this one turns it on a single sweaty man, and the comedy here isn't in spite of the craft, it's built from it. Rose sets a marimba walking figure loose over a broken-waltz pulse at a brisk clip, and the arrangement earns its jokes with timing: the stop-start accents on 'Peripheral. Accidental. Purely educational' land like a man caught red-handed, and the whole thing pivots on that guilty deadpan of a grown man who'll 'study grout / Like what he noticed four feet before.' The mock-anthropology of verse two ('First you cover for the weather. Then you decorate the hide') is where the writing sharpens into something Randy Newman would recognize, an unreliable narrator dressing up base instinct as social theory. The 5/4 instrumental break is the record's real flex, marimba restating the motif in a 3+2 stagger while the flute exaggerates the strut and the guitar answers crooked, before the final bar snaps abruptly back to a straight-4/4 chorus. If the bit runs slightly long, the outro's catalog of 'feathers, beads, buckles, buttons' testing your patience before the final 'Nice grout, by the way' pays it off, the way a good closing line pays off a shaggy joke. Proof Rose can make a novelty conceit hold real musical weight, and one more voice in the album's gallery of men caught mid-excuse.
