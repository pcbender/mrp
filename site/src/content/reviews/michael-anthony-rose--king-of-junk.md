With the doctorate in hand, the man goes public, and King Of Junk is the album's sly Trojan horse: a glam-funk jingle so infectious you almost miss that it's a meditation on notoriety and self-erasure. Over a strutting F-minor groove, all wah guitar and slap bass and brass stabs punctuated by literal trash-can hits, Rose builds Dr. Slump into a monument of pure repetition. The genius is in the couplets that undercut the swagger: "A sneer is praise with sharpened teeth / It still puts something underneath," landing on the chilling little thesis, "A man ignored is nearly dead / A man despised is widely read." The dry contralto turn from the Nanny, recalling how he asked "how his smile appeared / Not whether any child was near," is the moment the mask slips and the whole thing goes quietly cold, the same witness who has watched him since the cradle. The Sondheim-by-way-of-Steely-Dan showmanship earns its five minutes, though the game-show outro tips its hand a touch hard for a song that had already made the point. Arriving here, it's the record's most quotable and most quietly damning cut, and the bright hook is what lets the poison in.
