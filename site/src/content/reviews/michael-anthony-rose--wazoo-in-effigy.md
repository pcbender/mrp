The title track waits until the very end, and it makes you glad it did. Rose has spent 2026 pivoting hard, from the diarist of 'I'm Still Here' to the political sprawl of 'Outlandia,' but nothing in the catalog quite prepares you for this: a full electric orchestra deployed as courtroom, with the critic himself in the dock. The marimba pulse and fragmented brass stabs open in a jittery 7/8 before the whole thing lurches into a 4/4 chorus at a driving clip, and the tension between those meters is the point, an ensemble that keeps snapping shut on itself, abrupt stops leaving the baritone exposed. The genius here is structural: the song reads like a demolition of a man ('Technique, certainly. Feeling? I doubt it') until the third verse quietly turns the knife, 'Three hundred dollars to make both the records; ten thousand later for hired-in men,' and you realize the target may deserve some of this and none of it. Then the bridge lands the real gut-punch, the same wild-side hero who called Wazoo 'the single most untalented person I've heard in my life' standing at a podium twenty years later to induct him, and Rose's flat refrain, 'No correction was printed. Corrections are not printed,' says everything about who actually holds power in the room. After an album that put the critic's dismissals in a con man's mouth back on "Knurled Spanner," this closer settles the score directly, and the closing trumpet solo tears through the shifting meters before that dry, bored spoken 'Talentless' cuts it dead, an ending that would feel like a stunt if it hadn't been earned across everything before it. It asks a lot of a listener, and the density means it reveals itself over several passes, but the craft is unmistakable. One of the most daring things he's put his name to.
