King Of Junk's game-show outro was a tease; Wheel Of Frustration makes good on it, a five-minute concept piece built like a game show that eats its contestants alive. The clavinet-and-brass funk sits at a brisk clip in E major, all polished television sheen, until every chorus snaps its neck: the ensemble chirps "The house takes it back / Smile into the lens" while the band curdles underneath, and the host lands each round with a flat "Fuck you, it's mine." Rose stacks the cruelty by degrees, Mara crawling for ten thousand, Cal made to name his son on camera three, until June arrives with "No debts displayed, no stunt prepared" and the music nearly vanishes, the round simply called "Mine." The masterstroke is the coda: the whole apparatus strips to a sparse piano and a Nanny's contralto watching from the wings, "He never watched the people leave / He watched the little red light," then a tape-drag fade of a man playing recorded applause to an empty studio. It reaches for a lot, and the sheer density means it asks more of you than the plainspoken confessions elsewhere; this is a grower, a piece for the faithful that reveals its architecture on the third or fourth pass. But the ideas are real, and that hollow ending earns its chill.
