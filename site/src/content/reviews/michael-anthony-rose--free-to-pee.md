This is the third character sketch in a row, and by now you can feel the album's central comic engine humming: here is Rose the comic who has been hiding inside Rose the diarist all along, and "Free To Pee" is a full-blown one-act farce set to a brisk 3/4 that never once invites you to waltz. The whole thing hangs on the tension between Ted's escalating legal defense and Linda, whose entire vocabulary is a barked "TED!" landing on a sax-and-synth stab: the negotiation, the physics, the improved trajectory, all delivered with the wounded dignity of a man arguing before a court that does not exist. The doo-wop chorus turning martial by the second pass is the joke doing double duty, and the bridge is where it earns its keep, when the band drops to organ and bass and Ted goes philosopher: "That's water, patience, gravity, the original authority," then "Fifty-eight has humbled better men than me." There is real craft in how the outro cuts everything dead and loops back to that opening "Damn. Be right back," a punchline that reframes the whole five minutes as a bit Ted has clearly run before. It is broad, and coming after two comparably staged farces a few of the stop-start verses run longer than the gag strictly needs, so this is one for the room rather than the headphones; but as a piece of theater it holds the floor and knows exactly what it is.
