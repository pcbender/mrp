Following the factory intake of the opener, the manufacturing metaphor gives way to inheritance, and the pivot feels earned: inherited inadequacy rendered as a family recipe, "one part silence, two parts shame, a pinch of never good enough," itemized so flatly it lands harder than any wail would. Rose keeps the baritone quietly resigned through a brisk 4/4 in a major key, letting the arrangement do the arguing as it climbs from fingerpicked hush into something thick and weighted by the final chorus. The masterstroke is that nursery-rhyme bridge, "ring around the rosy" turning plague-sick and unsettling right before the last swell, a trick that recalls the way Roger Waters weaponized childhood singsong, and one the album will return to later. If the big finish leans a touch more anthemic than the material's cold clarity strictly needs, that's a small tax on a genuinely sharp idea. A highlight.
