This is where the record finds its center of gravity. Two tracks in, Rose zooms his lens down to a single absurd ordinance and holds it there until it's monstrous. He builds it at a laid-back pace in a minor key, then keeps knocking the floor out from under you: the crooked figures in the verses, the full-band choke that swallows the line before 'you're looking at an auxiliary clamp,' the way straight 4/4 keeps getting sabotaged by odd bars in the chorus so you never quite settle. The genius is in the delivery, that dry baritone talk-sing reading bureaucratic nonsense with a straight face while a cheerful civic choir chirps 'Clamp it down, citizen, nice and snug' like it's a jingle you're meant to whistle. The doo-wop bridge is where the joke turns cold: Eddie starts to say the property tax 'seemed,' a metallic snap cuts him off, and the sweet backing hum that answers is the most sinister thing on the record. It runs close to six minutes and the mid-section instrumental break, all angular theme traded around in shifting meter, asks patience of anyone who came for the hook. But the payoff, 'Praise be unto the Municipal Tongue Clamp' over a grotesque pageant that collapses into a tiny lounge combo cooing 'Perfect,' earns it. Randy Newman's satirical bite crossed with the odd-meter mischief of prog-jazz, this is the strongest thing here, and it holds the room.
