The clean arpeggios that open this one, ringing through chorus and delay, set a stillness the song keeps testing, and PCBender is patient about it: the D minor verses stay low and breathy, letting a line like "the smell of old things / new to me" hang before the floor drops out. When the power chords land on "darkness takes the shadows / the clock chimes twice / nothing but silence / calling my name," the payoff is the dynamic swing, verse-to-chorus contrast in the vein of a quieter Foo Fighters, and the baritone finds real grit at the top. At 129 and a full 4:35, the outro's wah-tinged solo and the pooling, thinning, gone imagery earn their length, though the repeated ad-libs stretch a little past where the tension peaks. A dependable cut that trusts its patience and mostly rewards it.
