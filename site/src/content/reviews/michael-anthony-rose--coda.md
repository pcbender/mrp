The record ends where the Overture whispered it would, with "Doctor, doctor, close your eyes" finally cashed out. "Coda" is a five-minute chamber requiem built around a morning briefing between a bored autocrat and the alto machine that runs his world. The genius is in the restraint: the piece keeps a cool, unhurried pulse in C minor, all low piano and celesta and space, so that when the internal voice finally admits "I chose four children breathing / over one dead woman's line," the confession lands with no cushion under it. Rose lets the domestic detail do the moral work, the two braids tied in government blue, the yellowed envelope worn soft at the fold, and the four-note motif from Just A Trophy that never quite completes until the celesta finishes it at the end, closing a thread the album planted forty minutes earlier. The sound design is a real accomplishment here, the shredder's clink and scrape, the children laughing past the mic, the flat verdict "Outlandia is secure," all of it earning that abrupt final silence. It asks patience and rewards it; the spoken briefing sections run a touch long before the emotional turn arrives, and this is not a hummable single but a piece to sit inside. A highlight, and exactly the ending this record needed: quiet, unresolved, and complete.
