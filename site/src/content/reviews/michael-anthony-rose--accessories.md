The title track's outward envy collapses back into the house, and 'Accessories' opens the album's domestic stretch with its coldest eye. Rose delivers a slow-motion domestic collapse in the flattest deadpan he's got, letting the brutality sit in the itemizing. "We told the neighbors we'd redone / the kitchen. Left the bedroom undone" is the whole marriage in two lines, and the clinical delivery makes it land harder than any wail would. At a brisk clip in a major key, the arrangement pulls a nice bait-and-switch: brittle, hushed intro into a punchy full-band verse, then choruses that gloss up with distorted guitar and gang doubles so the "family dream" sounds exactly as lacquered as it's supposed to. That gap between the shine and the seam, between the anthemic lift and "nobody says the word out loud," is where the song does its best work; it's Randy Newman's cold eye set to a heartland pulse. A highlight, and one of Rose's sharpest pieces of writing this year.
