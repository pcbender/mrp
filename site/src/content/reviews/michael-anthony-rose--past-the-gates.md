The manufactured grievance of the last track becomes a mob, and Rose builds an insurrection in real time: news feeds clipped and overlapping, a baritone negotiator working on the man who reads the count, a shouted mob refrain of "Past the gates!" over pounding barrier percussion in F# minor, all of it charging at a hardcore-punk clip. The through-composed structure refuses pop symmetry on purpose, and it earns its restlessness; the shift into 7/8 for the standoff ("Stand back / We own the building") lands as genuine disorientation rather than showing off. The masterstroke sits dead center, when everything cuts out for a plain contralto reciting the domestic ordinary: "Four children ate at half past one / One left the crust beside the plate." That single verse, the wiped milk, the refused winter shoes, the television turned down while the doors are stormed, does more work than the entire riot around it, letting Rose set catastrophe against the small logistics of a Tuesday. It shares some DNA with the theatrical dread of Bowie's Outside or Nine Inch Nails at their most narrative. At five and a half minutes and this dense, it demands a full sit; the wall of processed, tightly compressed sound can bury the finer lines on first pass. But the closing chorus turning the mechanism inward, "They won the thing they wanted / By breaking the mechanism," then the flat "Now turn it off," is chilling in exactly the way he intends, and it hands the next track its command. A grower, and one for the listener willing to meet it on its own difficult terms.
