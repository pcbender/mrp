4Castle has been circling political dread for a while, but this is the first time they've aimed the lens this directly at a specific kind of power, and the restraint makes it hit harder than a screed would. The verses build their case with dry economy: 'He only sleeps by mistake,' 'the deep end's dry, the tiles are cold,' images that feel absurdist and precise at the same time, the way good political writing does when it trusts the audience. The chorus lands in E major at a brisk 136 BPM, the harmony vocals opening up the room right when the lyric needs the feeling of collective exhalation, and the Hammond on rotary pushes it into something that recalls late-period Traffic or the more purposeful end of prog-rock civics. The spoken interlude, a quietly mumbling figure congratulating himself on being 'better than that,' is the sharpest move on the track, unsettling precisely because it sounds bored and sincere rather than theatrical. The final chorus earns its single-word substitution: 'rage goes quiet,' 'stood their ground,' traffic instead of shouting, ordinary life returned. That pivot is the whole argument, and 4Castle makes it without flinching.
