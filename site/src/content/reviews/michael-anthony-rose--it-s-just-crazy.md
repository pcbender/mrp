"People like crazy," Neo-Cons signed off, and this track builds the whole rally on it: a demagogue's operating manual set to garage-revival organ-rock, in G minor at a hard-charging clip. A high-baritone lead who names the grievance, tells the joke, then hands the crowd its lines, while a supporter ensemble answers on cue. The genius is structural, the way the pre-choruses shrink into clipped commands ("Name them / Shame them / Make it repeat") and Verse 3 collapses into pure call-and-response, the crowd finishing the leader's sentences before he can. Donin's bass-baritone recitative, "Persuasion asks the listener / to compare what he has heard. / Activation gives the listener / a task before the next word," is the thesis stated plainly, and it lands like the moment the curtain drops on the whole trick. If there's a cost, it's that the sheer density of ideas can outrun a first listen; this one rewards you the third time through, once you catch that the hook itself, "Crazy sounds true in a room," is diagnosing the very thing it's doing to you. A near-five-minute one-act play that never loses its footing, closing on the quietest, coldest line of all: "The one they cannot leave / without losing themselves." It holds the room, uneasily, which is exactly the point.
