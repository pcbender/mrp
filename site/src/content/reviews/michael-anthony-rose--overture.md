As an opening statement, "Overture" tells you exactly what kind of record you're holding: theatrical, unsettled, willing to weaponize a brass fanfare. It runs on that five-note fanfare and a pipe organ, then keeps yanking the rug, the stately E-minor 4/4 giving way to the crooked lurch of the 6/8 sections, calliope and distorted guitar colliding like a parade float that's come off its rails. The genius is in the phrasing of the slogans, "One nation under management / One future under seal," clipped and cheerful in a way that lands harder than any shout could. Rose (and the full ensemble he conjures here) builds the whole thing toward that final drop, where the bombast cuts out and a ticking pulse carries "Everything is yours - on borrowed time" into the whispered "Doctor, doctor, close your eyes," a music-box coda that reframes everything before it and, it turns out, seeds a motif this album will return to at its very end. It's dense, more spectacle than song, and the sheer scale means the emotional intimacy Rose is known for gets a little buried under the pageantry. But as scene-setting goes, this holds the room and dares you to keep listening.
